Welcome to MyGridNZ

The independent resource charting in real time the decarbonisation of New Zealand electricity.

Aotearoa New Zealand already generates most of its electricity from renewable sources — hydro, geothermal, and wind — but gas still plays a balancing role and the transition to 100% clean power is not yet complete. How much can expanded wind, solar and battery storage displace the remaining fossil generation? Can New Zealand keep the lights on through dry years and low-wind periods? How can New Zealand use its clean electricity advantage to decarbonise transport and industry?

MyGridNZ charts all of this change. It tells you how much electricity is produced, where that electricity comes from and how much carbon is being emitted — all in real time so you can come back again and again to see how New Zealand is tracking against clean power targets. Conversion & grid losses

Not all energy becomes electricity. Gas turbines convert around 57% of fuel into power; geothermal stations around 15%; biomass stations around 39%. The rest escapes as waste heat. A further 6% is lost in transmission once electricity reaches the grid. The diagram traces every unit of energy from source to delivered electricity, in real time.

Decarbonising New Zealand Electricity

The carbon intensity of New Zealand electricity has fallen over recent years, driven by growth in wind and solar alongside geothermal and hydro. The chart tracks this progress — and how far there is still to go to meet the 2030 clean power target.
